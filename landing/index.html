Why we're building this

)
A quieter way to follow the internet

We started readspace because we missed choosing what to read. Feeds used to be simple: you picked the writers you trusted, and their work arrived in order.

Then reading became something platforms optimise on our behalf. We wanted a reader where the sources, the order and the attention are yours — and one you can inspect, export or host yourself.

Nothing here ranks what you follow. The briefing exists for the days you only have ten minutes, not to replace the reading.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

Is it really free?
Yes. Basic covers ten feeds, the full source database, and the apps on every platform, with no card required. Pro is $5.99 a month if you want unlimited AI features, newsletters and two briefings a day.
Where is my reading data stored?
On readspace servers if you use the hosted apps, and nowhere else — no ads, no trackers, no third-party analytics on your reading. Self-host and it never leaves your machine.
Can I self-host it?
The whole platform runs on your own server for $0 with Docker. Bring your own API keys, then point the mobile apps and the extension at your instance.
Can I bring my subscriptions from Feedly or Reeder?
Export an OPML file from your current reader and import it in one step. You can export yours from readspace the same way, any time.
Where does AI get used?
Only where you ask for it: the daily briefing, article summaries, highlights and translation. Nothing is ever ranked or reordered by a model — your feed stays chronological.
What about paywalled articles?
readspace shows whatever the publisher puts in the feed. Where a site only syndicates an excerpt, you read that excerpt and open the original in one tap.
Still wondering something? Ask in the GitHub discussions.
